\chapter*{Overview}

This blueprint documents a machine-checked Lean~4 model of the Wikifunctions / WikiLambda object
system, built \emph{ground-up from the spec}. Its organising principle: \textbf{every value is a
\fnmodel{Z1/ZObjects}{\textsf{Z1}}/ZObject}, and the type layer is \emph{generated} from the authoritative schemata rather
than hand-written. The model lives under \repo{Wikifunctions/Model}{\texttt{Wikifunctions/Model/}}
and is Mathlib-free.

Only the \wf{Z1}/ZObject inductive is hand-written --- the uniform \emph{normal form} the
evaluator consumes, in which the \wf{Z6}/\wf{Z9} terminals and \wf{Z881} lists are all shapes of
\texttt{node} (Chapter~\ref{ch:zobject}). The compact \emph{canonical form} we store and transmit
is a separate type, related to normal form by total conversions (Chapter~\ref{ch:canonical}).
Every other built-in type is emitted by a compiler from its definition (Chapter~\ref{ch:types}).
Each node links to its exact Lean source and to the Function-model section or function-schemata
file it is derived from; no structural or behavioural claim is made without that spec citation.

\paragraph{The authorities cited.} The prose
\href{https://www.wikifunctions.org/wiki/Wikifunctions:Function_model}{Function model} (concepts),
the machine-enforced
\href{https://gitlab.wikimedia.org/repos/abstract-wiki/wikifunctions/function-schemata}{function-schemata}
(structural validity), and the live ZObjects (via the \texttt{wikilambda\_fetch} API, the ground
truth for community types). Where the model makes a simplification the spec leaves open, it is
marked \emph{(editorial)}.

\chapter{Z1/ZObjects --- the hand-written substrate}\label{ch:zobject}

The whole model rests on one inductive type and a few accessors, transcribed directly from the
spec's definition of a ZObject and its normal form.

\begin{definition}[ZObject]\label{def:zobject}
  \lean{Wikifunctions.Model.ZObject}\leanok
  Derived verbatim from \fnmodel{Z1/ZObjects}{Function model \S~Z1/ZObjects} (``A ZObject consists
  of a list of Key/value pairs'') in the spec's \fnmodel{Normal_form}{\S~Normal form} --- the
  \emph{uniform} representation the evaluator consumes (``Normal forms \ldots ensure that the input
  for evaluation is always uniform \ldots and requires a minimal amount of special cases''). A
  \texttt{ZObject} is a raw \texttt{str} leaf or a \texttt{node} --- an ordered list of key/value
  pairs. Two constructors; every type (\wf{Z6}, \wf{Z9}, \wf{Z4}, \wf{Z8}, \wf{Z7}, and \wf{Z881}
  lists) is a shape of \texttt{node}. Source: \leansrc{Wikifunctions/Model/ZObject.lean}{35}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Accessors and terminals]\label{def:accessors}
  \uses{def:zobject}
  \lean{Wikifunctions.Model.ZObject.typeId?}\leanok
  Key lookup (\texttt{get?}); the type ZID (\texttt{typeId?}; §Z1/ZObjects: ``every ZObject must
  have a key Z1K1/type''); and the terminals as smart constructors --- \texttt{string}~\(= \{\)
  \wf{Z1}K1{:}\wf{Z6}, \wf{Z6}K1{:}\(s\}\) and \texttt{reference}~\(= \{\)\wf{Z1}K1{:}\wf{Z9},
  \wf{Z9}K1{:}\(zid\}\), with recognizers \texttt{stringValue?}/\texttt{referenceId?}. The raw
  \texttt{str} leaf is only ever a terminal payload or a type-tag name.
  Source: \leansrc{Wikifunctions/Model/ZObject.lean}{103}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Z881 typed lists]\label{def:lists}
  \uses{def:accessors}
  \lean{Wikifunctions.Model.ZObject.typedList}\leanok
  Per \fnmodel{Normal_form}{§Normal form}, ``all Lists are represented as ZObjects, not as
  arrays'': a \wf{Z881}/Typed list is a cons-list of nodes --- head \texttt{K1}, tail \texttt{K2},
  and a \wf{Z7} call to \wf{Z881} for the element type --- so it needs no special constructor, only
  the smart builder \texttt{typedList}. (The compact array rendering lives in
  Chapter~\ref{ch:canonical}.) Source: \leansrc{Wikifunctions/Model/ZObject.lean}{148}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Faithful to the spec's number 2]\label{lem:nattwo}
  \uses{def:accessors}
  \lean{Wikifunctions.Model.ZObject.natTwo}\leanok
  \texttt{natTwo} reproduces the \fnmodel{Normal_form}{§Normal-form} example \emph{definitionally}
  (\texttt{rfl}): the number 2 is \texttt{\{Z1K1:\{Z1K1:Z9,\,Z9K1:Z10\},
  Z10K1:\{Z1K1:Z6,\,Z6K1:"2"\}\}}. The load-bearing faithfulness check for the substrate.
  Source: \leansrc{Wikifunctions/Model/ZObject.lean}{155}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{definition}[Well-formedness]\label{def:wf}
  \uses{def:zobject}
  \lean{Wikifunctions.Model.ZObject.WellFormed}\leanok
  The inductive is deliberately permissive --- as the dynamically-typed deployed system is, where
  validators can be skipped --- so validity is a \emph{separate, decidable} predicate \texttt{wf}:
  a raw \texttt{str} is not a standalone ZObject, and a \texttt{node} is well-formed iff it is a
  \wf{Z6}/\wf{Z9} terminal shape or a general object with a \wf{Z1}K1 key, no duplicate keys
  (§Z1/ZObjects: ``every Key can only appear once''), and every value well-formed. \texttt{natTwo},
  the terminals, and a \texttt{typedList} check well-formed; a bare leaf and a \wf{Z1}K1-less node
  are rejected. Source: \leansrc{Wikifunctions/Model/ZObject.lean}{201}.
\end{definition}

\chapter{Canonical form and the conversions}\label{ch:canonical}

Normal form is uniform but verbose. ``In order to make ZObjects more readable and more compact, we
usually store and transmit them in the so-called canonical form'' (\fnmodel{Canonical_form}{§Canonical
form}). Canonical form is a \emph{separate} type, related to normal form by total conversions.

\begin{definition}[Canonical form]\label{def:canonical}
  \lean{Wikifunctions.Model.Canonical}\leanok
  A \texttt{Canonical} value is compact JSON: a \texttt{str}, an \texttt{arr}, or an \texttt{obj}.
  Canonical form applies exactly three collapses (\fnmodel{Canonical_form}{§Canonical form}): a
  \wf{Z6}/String to a bare string, a \wf{Z9}/Reference to a bare ZID, and a \wf{Z881}/Typed list to
  a \emph{Benjamin array} --- a JSON array whose \textbf{first element is the element type} and
  whose rest are the items (\texttt{["Z6","a","b"]}). Source:
  \leansrc{Wikifunctions/Model/Canonical.lean}{26}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[canonicalize / normalize]\label{def:conversions}
  \uses{def:canonical, def:lists}
  \lean{Wikifunctions.Model.Canonical.canonicalize}\leanok
  \texttt{canonicalize} (normal~\(\to\)~canonical) collapses the terminals and flattens each
  \wf{Z881} cons-list into its Benjamin array; \texttt{normalize} (canonical~\(\to\)~normal)
  inverts this, reading a bare ZID-shaped string as a \wf{Z9} reference and a Benjamin array as a
  cons-list. Machine-checked both ways, including on the spec's number~2 and on
  \texttt{["Z6","a","b"]}, with round-trips \texttt{normalize~\(\circ\)~canonicalize} and
  \texttt{canonicalize~\(\circ\)~normalize}. \emph{(editorial)} \texttt{canonicalize} recognises a
  list by the cons key-order our builders emit. Source:
  \leansrc{Wikifunctions/Model/Canonical.lean}{91}.
\end{definition}

\chapter{The generated type layer}\label{ch:types}

Above the substrate, the built-in types are not written by hand --- they are compiled from their
definitions, so the type layer is faithful to the schemata by construction.

\begin{definition}[The type compiler]\label{def:codegen}
  \uses{def:zobject}
  \repo{codegen/gen\_type.py}{\texttt{codegen/gen\_type.py}} reads each type's authoritative
  definition --- the function-schemata \schema{Z4}-style YAML for built-ins, or the live \wf{Z4}
  (\texttt{wikilambda\_fetch}) for community types, both reduced to \(\{\)type ZID, ordered data
  keys\(\}\) --- and emits, on the \texttt{ZObject} substrate, a smart constructor, per-key
  accessors, an \texttt{isZxx} recognizer, and a \texttt{by decide} self-check that the constructor
  and recognizer agree. The schema \emph{is} the spec, so generation removes per-type
  interpretation entirely.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The built-in inventory]\label{def:types}
  \uses{def:codegen}
  All \textbf{29 built-in types} --- every function-schemata \texttt{CANONICAL} type except the
  hand-written \wf{Z1}/\wf{Z6}/\wf{Z9} --- are generated into
  \repo{Wikifunctions/Model/Types}{\texttt{Wikifunctions/Model/Types/}} (\wf{Z2}, \wf{Z3}, \wf{Z4},
  \wf{Z5}, \wf{Z7}, \wf{Z8}, \wf{Z14}, \wf{Z16}, \wf{Z17}, \wf{Z18}, \wf{Z22}, \wf{Z40}, \ldots).
  All from the schemata YAML except \wf{Z23}/Nothing (live \wf{Z4} fallback); all build green with
  their self-checks. The community numeric type \wf{Z19677}/Rational is generated the same way from
  its live \wf{Z4}. \emph{(editorial)} The current \texttt{isZxx} checks key \emph{presence}; the
  schema's enum/type constraints (e.g.\ \wf{Z40}K1 \(\in \{\)\wf{Z41}, \wf{Z42}\(\}\)) are the next
  increment.
\end{definition}
